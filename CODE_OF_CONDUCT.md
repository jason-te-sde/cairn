# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be straightforward and be kind. Argue with the code, not the person who wrote it.

## What is expected

- **Assume the other person had a reason.** Ask what it was before explaining why it was wrong.
- **Criticise the change, not the author.** "This loses an effect if the process dies here" is
  useful. "This is sloppy" is not.
- **Say what you are unsure about.** A review that distinguishes "this is wrong" from "I do not
  follow this" is worth more than one that does not.
- **Accept a "no" with a reason.** `docs/design/` exists so that a rejected idea has an argument
  attached rather than a mood.

## What is not

Harassment, personal attacks, insults, dismissiveness about someone's experience level, sexualised
language or imagery, publishing private information, and sustained disruption.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and anywhere someone is representing this
project.

## Enforcement

Report to **te.r@northeastern.edu**. Reports are read by the maintainer and kept private.

Responses, in escalating order: a private conversation, a request to change or withdraw a comment, a
temporary block, a permanent block. A single serious incident can go straight to the end of that
list.

The maintainer is held to the same standard, and is not the right person to report the maintainer
to; in that case, GitHub's own reporting is the route.
